## Corrected figures

1. Packaging waste
   - Brief claim: `14.5M+ tons annually from meal kits`
   - Better-supported figure: `>1.5 billion pounds` of packaging and food waste from U.S. meal-kit sales in 2020.
   - Conversion: about `750,000 tons`.
   - Correction: the brief's figure appears inflated by roughly `19x` and likely conflates meal kits with a much larger food-packaging category.

2. Cost per serving
   - Brief claim: `meal kits $10-$15+` vs. `groceries $4-$7`
   - Better-supported figure: current official starter pricing is closer to `Blue Apron from $7.99` and `HelloFresh from $9.99` before the user optimizes for promotions, plan size, or shipping.
   - Correction: the meal-kit side of the brief is directionally right but too coarse. The grocery benchmark is not strongly established by primary data in this pass; USDA data suggests a lower at-home floor than `$4-$7` for cost-disciplined households, but real grocery baskets can be higher depending on ingredients and waste.

3. HelloFresh trajectory
   - Brief implication: large player still in straightforward growth mode
   - Better-supported figure: HelloFresh FY2024 revenue was down `0.9%` constant currency, with active customers down to `6.64M` from `6.93M`. By 9M 2025, revenue was down `8.2%` constant currency, orders down `7.6%`, meals down `7.3%`, and active customers down to `6.61M`.
   - Correction: HelloFresh is managing profitability during a volume contraction, not posting clean growth.

4. U.S. market size
   - Brief implication: one clear, stable TAM number exists for 2024-2026
   - Better-supported figure: at least one published estimate puts the U.S. meal-kit market at `$6.68B` in 2024.
   - Inference: applying Grand View's 17.7% CAGR implies about `$7.86B` in 2025 and `$9.25B` in 2026.
   - Correction: treat 2025-2026 TAM as scenario math from analyst forecasts, not as a settled market fact.

## Unverifiable claims

1. A credible, current primary-source number for meal-kit-specific annual cancellation or churn rates.
2. A strong public dataset tying packaging waste directly to meal-kit cancellation, rather than to generic post-purchase dissatisfaction.
3. Public revenue or paying-user counts for the broader "recipe app" segment, including Samsung Food, Paprika, and AnyList.
4. A reliable public benchmark for meal-planning app retention duration.
5. A single authoritative U.S. meal-kit market-size number for 2025-2026. Available figures are analyst estimates and differ by methodology.

## Surprising findings

1. The packaging-waste number in the brief is the biggest factual problem. The best current public figure I found is `~0.75M tons`, not `14.5M+ tons`.
2. The strongest market signal is not runaway meal-kit growth. It is incumbents defending margins while orders, meals, and customers soften.
3. Blue Apron is no longer a healthy stand-alone comparator; it was acquired in November 2023.
4. Public evidence does not support using packaging waste as the lead cancellation narrative. Cost, flexibility, and shopping preferences appear more consistently documented.
5. The adjacent recipe-planning space is more crowded than the brief suggests, but most of the economics are private, which makes market-size claims about that sub-segment hard to verify cleanly.
