# Research Prompt 01 — Market Validation
**Platform:** Gemini Deep Research (primary) | Grok (real-time market data) | ChatGPT, Claude, BigPickle, Raptor Mini, MiniMax (cross-check)
**Topic:** Meal kit market size, packaging waste claims, cost-per-serving benchmarks

---

## Project Context

Stàged is a free PWA kitchen coordination platform targeting households exhausted by meal kit subscriptions. It replaces physical kit delivery with AI-assisted recipe planning, real-time household grocery list coordination, and one-tap grocery fulfillment via Instacart/Amazon Fresh. Revenue comes from fulfillment affiliate commissions, programmatic ingredient placement, and live chef events.

The product is positioned against HelloFresh/Blue Apron (physical kits), Samsung Food/Whisk (recipe apps without eco positioning), and Paprika/AnyList (no delivery integration). Core differentiator: zero-waste positioning, offline-first PWA, household coordination.

---

## Claims to Verify

The following claims appear in the product brief and need sourced verification:

1. "14.5M+ tons of packaging waste annually from meal kits" — source unknown, may be conflated with total US food packaging waste
2. "Meal kit cost-per-serving: $10–$15+" vs. "grocery shopping: $4–$7 per serving"
3. "Meal kit fatigue is real and growing" — subscription cancellation rates, churn data
4. HelloFresh and Blue Apron market share and recent financial trajectory (growth or decline?)
5. The US meal kit market total addressable market size (2024–2026)
6. Consumer sentiment on meal kit packaging waste — is this a meaningful purchase driver or post-purchase rationalization?

---

## Research Questions

1. What is the actual volume of packaging waste attributed specifically to meal kit services (not total food packaging)?
2. What are the most credible sources for meal kit cost-per-serving data (company disclosures, USDA, consumer research)?
3. What is the current HelloFresh subscriber count and recent subscriber trajectory (growth/decline 2023–2026)?
4. What are documented meal kit cancellation/churn rates from primary sources (earnings calls, consumer surveys)?
5. Is there consumer research data specifically linking packaging concerns to meal kit cancellation?
6. What is the US home cooking trend trajectory post-2022 — are people cooking more or reverting to delivery?
7. What is the market size of the "recipe app" segment (Paprika, AnyList, Samsung Food) — paying users, revenue?
8. Are there household grocery planning apps with meaningful market share not mentioned in the brief?
9. What consumer segments over-index on eco-conscious food purchasing behavior — age, income, geography?
10. Is there published data on "meal planning app" user retention rates — how long do users stick with meal planning tools?

---

## Output Format

Please respond with:
1. **Verified claims** — claim, source, confidence (high/medium/low)
2. **Corrected figures** — where the brief's numbers differ from sourced data
3. **Unverifiable claims** — claims you couldn't find sourced data for
4. **Surprising findings** — anything that contradicts the product's market assumptions
5. **Source list** — publication, date, URL or citation for each data point used

---

## Research Standards

- Prefer primary sources first: official docs, earnings releases, API docs, legal text, platform policies, and first-party pricing pages.
- Separate sourced facts from inference. If you calculate or extrapolate a number, label it clearly as an inference.
- Include exact dates for time-sensitive claims and use current data where available.
- For every material claim, provide a URL or a formal citation.
- If a claim cannot be verified from credible public sources, say that explicitly instead of filling the gap with plausible language.
- If sources conflict, show the range and explain why the estimates may differ.
- Do not repeat assumptions from the brief unless they were independently verified in this research pass.
