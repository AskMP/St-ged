# Research Prompt 06 — Monetization Benchmarks
**Platform:** Gemini Deep Research (primary) | Grok (live CPG spend data, current affiliate program news) | ChatGPT, Claude, BigPickle, Raptor Mini, MiniMax (cross-check)
**Topic:** Featured ingredient placement CPM/CPC, recipe affiliate revenue reality, food app monetization benchmarks

---

## Project Context

Stàged's revenue model has three pillars:
1. **Fulfillment commissions** — 3–7% affiliate fee from Instacart/Amazon Fresh per order
2. **Programmatic product placement** — "Featured Ingredients" integrated into recipes (e.g., "Use Kerrygold Butter for best results")
3. **Stàge Events** — live cooking sessions at $5–$15 entry fee

The product is free to users. No subscription. All revenue from partnerships and events. This model needs to be stress-tested: are these revenue rates realistic? At what scale does this become sustainable? What are comparable products earning?

---

## Claims to Verify

1. Fulfillment affiliate commissions of "3–7%" are achievable from Instacart/Amazon Fresh
2. "Featured ingredient" programmatic placement is a viable, non-intrusive ad format with meaningful CPM
3. Live cooking events at $5–$15 are a viable high-margin revenue stream
4. A free, ad-supported food app can reach sustainability without a subscription paywall

---

## Research Questions

1. What commission rates do recipe/food apps actually earn from Instacart affiliate programs — are published rates available from Instacart's partner documentation or news coverage?
2. Are there public revenue figures or case studies from food/recipe apps that monetize via grocery affiliate links (Whisk, BigOven, Yummly, Mealime)?
3. What CPM/CPC rates are typical for contextual advertising in food/recipe content (not banner ads — editorial-style product placement)?
4. Are there food/CPG brands that run "featured ingredient" placement programs in recipe apps — who are they, what do they pay?
5. What is the average order value for a grocery delivery order via Instacart in the US (2024–2025) — this determines the dollar value of a 3–7% commission?
6. At what monthly active user count does a recipe + grocery affiliate model typically break even or generate meaningful revenue — are there published benchmarks?
7. What are the revenue economics of live culinary events (online cooking classes) — average ticket price, conversion rate, instructor cost structure?
8. How have ad-supported food content apps (Tasty, Delish, Yummly) performed financially — are they profitable without subscriptions?
9. What is the average revenue per user (ARPU) for food/recipe apps with affiliate monetization?
10. Are CPG brands (Kerrygold, Chosen Foods, Oatly, etc.) actively investing in recipe-integrated product placement in 2025 — where is this budget going?
11. What is the take rate that platforms like Goldbelly or Airbnb Experiences take from food/culinary events — relevant for benchmarking Stàge Events economics?

---

## Output Format

1. **Revenue model reality check** — each revenue stream with sourced benchmark data vs. assumed rates
2. **Break-even math** — estimated MAU needed for each stream to reach $1K/mo, $10K/mo, $100K/mo MRR
3. **CPG placement landscape** — which brands are spending in this channel and how
4. **Comparable app economics** — what publicly known food apps earn from similar models
5. **Risk flags** — which revenue assumptions are most fragile or most dependent on uncontrollable factors
6. **Source list** — earnings reports, partner program documentation, journalism, industry reports, dated

---

## Research Standards

- Prefer primary sources first: official docs, earnings releases, API docs, legal text, platform policies, and first-party pricing pages.
- Separate sourced facts from inference. If you calculate or extrapolate a number, label it clearly as an inference.
- Include exact dates for time-sensitive claims and use current data where available.
- For every material claim, provide a URL or a formal citation.
- If a claim cannot be verified from credible public sources, say that explicitly instead of filling the gap with plausible language.
- If sources conflict, show the range and explain why the estimates may differ.
- Do not repeat assumptions from the brief unless they were independently verified in this research pass.
