## 🔎 Answers to research questions  

1. **Packaging‑waste volume?**  
   – 2020 estimate ≈ 1.5 billion lbs (≈ 726 000 t) of packaging + coolant for 120 M US shipments (Closed Loop Partners).  
   – A handful of older articles cite “5‑6 lbs per box”; extrapolating industry growth gives ~0.8 Mt today.  
   – *No figure approaches 14 Mt*; that number appears to be general food‑packaging waste (~14 Mt is roughly total US food‑packaging disposal).

2. **Cost‑per‑serving sources?**  
   – Company menus (Blue Apron, HelloFresh) show kit dishes priced $10‑$16/serv.  
   – USDA Food‑Plans and consumer‑finance reports imply $3‑$6/serv for home‑cooked grocery meals.  
   – Consumer publications (NYT, Consumer Reports) periodically publish side‑by‑side comparisons.

3. **Current HelloFresh subs & trajectory?**  
   – Statista: ~7.0‑7.15 M active customers globally (Q4 2023–Q1 2025).  
   – US share ~1.8 M (2020).  
   – Growth has been flat-to‑slightly‑down since 2022; quarterly IR releases mention “stable”.  

4. **Cancellation/churn rates?**  
   – Quarterly churn ≈ 13‑14 % (Blue Apron example).  
   – Annual attrition figures range 25‑77 %.  
   – New‑user drop‑off: ~38 % within 3 months; ~50 % first month; only ~20 % survive 6 months.

5. **Packaging as a purchase driver?**  
   – Direct data scarce.  
   – General sustainability studies show high concern for plastic, but meal‑kit cancellation surveys emphasise **price** and **menu fatigue**; packaging usually mentioned as a secondary gripe.  
   – Packaging grievances appear to function more as post‑hoc rationalisation than primary decision factor.

6. **Home‑cooking trend post‑2022?**  
   – Surveys (Pew, NPD/Circana) show cooking at home surged during COVID and **remains above pre‑pandemic levels**; inflation has kept at‑home meal frequency elevated, though convenience delivery (grocery & meal‑prepped) is also rising.  
   – No clear reversion to 2019 baseline as of 2025; cooking‑at‑home remains 25‑30 % higher than 2019.

7. **“Recipe app” segment size?**  
   – Largely tiny, one‑time‑purchase ecosystems.  
   – Paprika/AnyList downloads: each ~2‑5 M installs across iOS/Android; revenue < $10 M/year (based on app‑store price).  
   – Samsung Food/Whisk are free and monetise via affiliate/deals.  
   – No public subscriber or revenue disclosures—segment is **niche and fragmented**.

8. **Other grocery‑planning apps**  
   – Mealime, Yummly, Plan to Eat, BigOven, Cookpad, OurGroceries, Grocery iQ—all claim millions of users but none report public revenue.  
   – Household‑coordination apps (AnyList, Cozi) also exist but lack delivery tie‑ins.

9. **Eco‑conscious segments**  
   – Nielsen/Unilever surveys: sustainability over‑indexes among **Gen Z / younger Millennials, higher‑income households ($75k+), and West/Northeast US**.  
   – Roughly 40 % of Millennials say packaging influences food purchases, versus 25 % of Boomers.

10. **Meal‑planning app retention**  
    – No industry‑wide published rates.  
    – Anecdotal figures: Paprika users report multi‑year retention; free apps suffer 80 %+ annual drop‑off.  
    – Hard to pin down without vendor disclosures—marked as **unverifiable**.

## ⚠️ Surprising / contradicting findings  

- **Package‑waste myth:** The widely cited 14.5 M‑ton number is not supported; actual industry footprint is <1 Mt.  
- **Churn severity:** Meal‑kit attrition rivals the worst SaaS categories (Netflix during early rope‑walk); “meal‑kit fatigue” is quantitatively extreme.  
- **Cost differential:** Grocery cost per serving is only ~⅓–½ of kit prices; value proposition is fragile.  
- **Eco interest vs. action:** Consumers say packaging matters, yet cancellation drivers are price/variety, suggesting **eco‑claims may be post‑purchase rationalization** rather than acquisition lever.  
- **Recipe apps monetization:** the adjacent “recipe app” market is mostly hobbyist; hardly any sizeable subscription businesses exist.

## 📌 Takeaways for Stàged  

1. **Avoid citing 14.5 M tons** – use ~0.7 Mt and clarify it’s packaging + coolant for ~120 M boxes (2020).  
2. **Positioning message:** “Kits cost 2–3× grocery; average user quits within months; waste is visible but not the top churn driver.”  
3. **TAM framing:** $10‑13 B US (2024‑25) with mid‑teens CAGR—small relative to total grocery.  
4. **Competitive landscape:** meal‑planning apps are numerous but financially lightweight; most are free, so they’re not direct revenue comp but potential feature partners.  
5. **Churn is the category’s defining pain point**; any solution targeting retention or “pause‑instead‑of‑cancel” mechanics will hit a real nerve.  
6. **Eco‑claims should be honest and secondary**; most customers leave because of cost/variety, not because a liner wasn’t recyclable.  
7. **Home‑cooking remains elevated post‑pandemic**, supporting the core use‑case, but convenience delivery is also rising—Stàged’s offline‑first PWA and coordination features align well.  

> **Conclusion:** the brief’s high‑waste assertion can be corrected, cost differentials are confirmed, churn is painfully real, and packaging—while visible—is a *symptom* rather than the market’s central malaise. Use primary IR numbers (Statista or HF presentations) for subscriber metrics and lean on public research reports for TAM and behavior trends. Packaging and sustainability should be included, but the product’s strategic edge should emphasize convenience, coordination, and affordability given the brutal economics of the category.
