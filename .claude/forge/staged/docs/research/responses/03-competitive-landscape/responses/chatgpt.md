## Validated gaps

1. `Party / potluck coordination` still looks open. I did not find a mainstream consumer product that clearly owns shared event menu planning plus grocery execution.
2. `Offline-first PWA positioning` still looks open. Competitors emphasize apps and services, but not resilient low-connectivity kitchen or in-store workflows as a core value proposition.
3. `Explicit zero-waste decision support` still looks open. Several competitors mention reducing waste, but I did not find a robust, measurable zero-waste workflow that is central to the product story.
4. `Household coordination plus live-event workflow` still looks open. Existing products cluster around planning, lists, or fulfillment rather than coordinated group cooking experiences.

## Closed gaps

1. `Samsung Food lacks household sharing` is false. It supports shared grocery lists.
2. `Samsung Food lacks delivery integration` is false. It claims `23` integrated retailers.
3. `Samsung Food has no eco positioning` is overstated. It is not zero-waste-first, but it does explicitly promise food-waste reduction.
4. `AnyList has no fulfillment` is false. AnyList already supports online shopping across major grocery partners, including Instacart and Amazon Fresh.
5. `Paprika / AnyList are the main non-kit digital comparators` is incomplete. Mealime, SideChef, and Cooklist are closer to Staged's workflow than the brief implies.

## Emerging threats

1. Samsung Food remains the most dangerous all-in-one consumer incumbent because it already combines planning, sharing, and retailer integration at scale.
2. Mealime is a direct strategic threat because it pairs meal planning with grocery delivery and food-waste reduction messaging.
3. SideChef is a serious commerce-layer threat because it already sells brand and retailer integrations around shoppable recipes.
4. Cooklist is an adjacent threat from the pantry and price-intelligence side of the market.
5. Yummly's shutdown on December 20, 2024 is also a warning signal: this category is crowded, and even heavily backed products can fail to sustain.
6. The "Meez" name now needs disambiguation. `getmeez.com` remains B2B restaurant software, while `meez.app` appears to be a separate consumer home-cooking product. The brief should not treat "Meez" as a single market actor.

## User voice

This section is directional rather than exhaustive. I did not run a full-scale review scrape.

1. Public community discussion around Paprika shows recurring demand for real family collaboration instead of one-user or shared-account workarounds.
2. Mealime's own help documentation still points users toward account-sharing patterns, which suggests household collaboration is not fully solved even in a product with strong shopping integrations.
3. AnyList's product messaging leans heavily on online shopping breadth, which implies retailer coverage and checkout convenience are core user pulls.
4. Across the market, the unmet need is not basic recipe storage. It is coordinated execution: who is cooking, who is buying, what is already in the pantry, and how that all stays in sync across a household.
